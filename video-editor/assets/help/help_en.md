=== getting-started
title: Getting started
section: Getting started
keywords: start, begin, first, new video, basics, tour, guide, overview, how it works, projects, rename, duplicate, delete project
related: importing, timeline, export, undo-and-saving

ClipForge puts your videos, photos and music together into one video.

1. On the project list, tap **New project**.
2. Tap **Add media** and pick videos or photos.
3. Arrange them on the timeline at the bottom: trim, split and move clips.
4. Add music, titles or captions if you like.
5. Tap **Export** at the top right to make the finished video.

Everything is saved by itself as you go.

On the project list, the three dots on a project give you **Rename**, **Duplicate**, **Share project file** and **Delete**.

Want the quick tour again? Open Help (the **?** at the top) and tap **Show the tour**.

=== importing
title: Adding videos and photos
section: Getting started
keywords: import, add, add media, pick, picker, gallery, files, video, photo, picture, image, drag, drop, drag and drop, folder, finder, explorer, open
related: getting-started, timeline, where-files-go, music

Tap **Add media** and choose one or more videos or photos. They go in after the selected clip, or at the end when no clip is selected.

On a phone you can also tap the **+** next to the play button and choose **Videos or photos**. The phone's own picker opens: choose from your gallery or your files.

## Drag and drop (computer)

You can drag videos, photos and music straight from a folder onto the ClipForge window.

- On the project list, the files start a new video.
- In the editor, they are added to the video. Drop them on the timeline to put them exactly between two clips.
- Drop a whole folder to add the files inside it, in name order.
- A project file (.clipforge.zip) dropped on the project list opens as a project.

Files ClipForge cannot use are listed, and the rest are added.

=== timeline
title: The timeline: select, trim and move clips
section: Editing
keywords: timeline, select, tap, trim, cut ends, shorten, handles, drag, move, reorder, order, arrange, delete clip, remove, zoom, playhead, move earlier, move later
related: split, undo-and-saving, speed, transitions, sound-tracks

The timeline at the bottom shows your clips in order. The white line is the playhead: what you see in the preview.

- **Select**: tap a clip. The preview jumps to it and the tools for that clip appear. Tap **Done** to go back.
- **Trim**: select a clip, then drag its left or right end to make it shorter.
- **Move**: hold a clip and drag it to a new place. You can also use **Move earlier** and **Move later** (on a phone, under **More**).
- **Delete**: select the clip and tap **Delete**.
- **Zoom**: the magnifier buttons next to the time make the timeline bigger or smaller.

Below the clips there is a row for each sound track (music, voice, effects), and one for titles and captions. Select and drag them the same way; a sound can also be dragged up or down onto another track. The names on the left are the rows; on a computer the small mute and solo buttons there switch a row off or play it alone.

=== split
title: Splitting a clip
section: Editing
keywords: split, cut, cut in two, scissors, divide, separate, chop, slice
related: timeline, remove-silences, freeze-frame

**Split** cuts the clip under the playhead into two pieces.

1. Move the playhead to the spot where you want the cut.
2. Tap **Split**.

Now you can move, trim or delete each piece on its own. On a computer, the **S** key does the same.

=== undo-and-saving
title: Undo, autosave and the save sign
section: Editing
keywords: undo, redo, mistake, go back, save, saved, saving, autosave, auto save, not saved, cloud, lost, storage full
related: history, storage, keyboard-shortcuts, getting-started

Made a mistake? Tap **Undo** (the curved arrow at the top). **Redo** brings the change back. Every change to the video can be undone; renaming the project is the one thing that cannot (just rename it again). To go back several steps at once, use **History**.

You never need to save. ClipForge saves every change by itself. The cloud sign at the top tells you how it is going:

- **Saved**: all your changes are safe on this device.
- **Saving…**: it is writing your latest change.
- **Not saved**: it could not save, for example because the storage is full. Tap it to try again. ClipForge will not let you leave the project without asking first.

=== history
title: History: go back to any change
section: Editing
keywords: history, changes, list of changes, steps, go back, earlier version, previous version, undo many, undo several, revert, restore, log, what did i change
related: undo-and-saving, keyboard-shortcuts

**History** lists every change you made since you opened the project, newest at the top, each with a short name ("Speed 1.25× on clip 2", "Removed 12 silences", "Voice focus 85% on clip 1") and the time.

- On a computer: click the clock-arrow button at the top, or choose **Edit** > **History** in the menu bar (Ctrl+Shift+H, on a Mac Cmd+Shift+H). The list opens on the right.
- On a phone: tap **More** in the bar at the bottom, then **History**.

Tap a step to go back to just after it. The step you are on is highlighted. Later steps stay in the list (in grey) until you make a new change, so you can jump forward again. Dragging a slider counts as one step.

The list starts again each time you open the project; your video itself is always saved.

=== speed
title: Speed and slow motion
section: Editing
keywords: speed, slow, slow motion, slowmo, fast, faster, quicker, time lapse, 2x, 0.5x, tempo
related: timeline, transitions, freeze-frame

Select a video clip and tap **Speed**.

- Choose a speed from 0.25× (very slow) to 4× (very fast), or use **Any speed** for something in between.
- Slow motion looks smoothest with videos filmed at a high frame rate.
- Voices keep their natural pitch, so speech sped up a little still sounds normal.

**Apply to all clips** gives every video the same speed.

=== transitions
title: Transitions between clips
section: Editing
keywords: transition, crossfade, cross fade, dissolve, fade, fade to black, fade through black, slide, wipe, effect between clips
related: timeline, speed

A transition changes smoothly from one clip to the next.

Select the second of the two clips. On a phone tap **More**, then **Transition**. On a computer open **Transition** in the panel on the right.

Choose **Crossfade**, **Fade through black**, **Slide left** or **Slide up**, and set the **Length**.

The two clips overlap while the transition plays, so the video gets a little shorter. The first clip has no transition, because nothing comes before it.

=== look
title: Look: brightness, colour and zoom
section: Picture
keywords: look, brightness, bright, dark, too dark, light, contrast, saturation, colour, color, colours, zoom, crop, closer, filter, reset look
related: fit-fill-blur, shape, freeze-frame, crop-position

Select a clip and tap **Look**.

- **Brightness** makes it lighter or darker. Good for rescuing dark footage.
- **Contrast** makes the difference between light and dark stronger or softer.
- **Saturation** makes colours stronger, or takes them away for black and white.
- **Zoom in** moves closer to the middle of the picture. To zoom into another part, move it with **Position** under **Crop**.

**Filter** gives a ready-made look in one tap, and **Warmth** makes the colours warmer or cooler (see Filters and warmth).

**Reset look** puts everything back. **Apply to all clips** gives every clip this look.

=== crop-position
title: Crop, rotate and position the picture
section: Picture
keywords: crop, cut edges, trim picture, rotate, turn, sideways, upside down, flip, mirror, position, move picture, pan, reframe, keep person in shot, vertical, portrait, tiktok, reels, 9:16, aspect, straighten
related: fit-fill-blur, shape, look, overlay

Select a clip and open **Crop**. On a computer it is a section of the panel on the right; on a phone tap **More**, then **Crop and position**. The whole picture shows on the preview with a frame over it.

- Drag the corners or sides of the frame to cut away what you do not want. Drag inside the frame to move it.
- The buttons **Free**, **Original**, **16:9**, **9:16**, **1:1** and **4:5** keep the frame in that shape while you drag.
- **Rotate left** and **Rotate right** turn the picture a quarter turn, for a video filmed sideways. **Flip sideways** and **Flip upside down** mirror it.
- **Position** (**Left – right** and **Up – down**) chooses which part of the picture stays in the frame when the picture is bigger than the frame: with **Fill** or when zoomed in.

**Reset crop and position** puts everything back. Every change is one step in **History**, and Undo takes it back.

## Vertical video with the person in shot

1. Set the shape to **9:16 upright** (the **Shape** button at the top).
2. Select the wide clip, open **Look** and choose **Fill**.
3. Open **Crop** and move **Left – right** until the person is in the middle.

The export looks exactly like the preview.

=== overlay
title: Overlay: picture-in-picture, side by side, cut-aways
section: Picture
keywords: overlay, picture in picture, pip, second video, two videos, video on video, split screen, side by side, top and bottom, camera angle, second camera, cut-away, cutaway, b-roll, webcam, podcast, reaction, layer, frame, border
related: crop-position, timeline, volume-and-mute

An overlay is a video or photo shown over your video, on its own row above the main clips.

1. Move the playhead to where it should start.
2. Tap **Overlay** (on a phone: the **+** next to Play, then **Overlay (picture-in-picture)**) and pick a video or photo.

It starts as a small picture in the bottom right corner. Select it to change it:

- **Layout**: picture-in-picture in a corner (choose the **Corner** and the **Size**), **Side by side** or **Top and bottom** (your video on one half, the overlay on the other), **Full screen** (it covers your video: good for a cut-away or another camera), or **Free position**.
- On the preview, drag a selected overlay to put it anywhere, and drag its corner to make it bigger or smaller. **White frame** draws a thin border around it.
- **Crop** works like for any clip: crop, rotate, flip and choose which part stays in its box.
- **Sound, speed and fades**: its volume (or **Mute overlay sound**), its speed, and how it fades in and out. Its sound plays together with the sound of your video.

On the overlay row, drag it to move it and drag its ends to trim it. Overlays never overlap each other: one dropped on another goes next to it. Titles and captions stay on top of overlays. Overlays stay where they are when you change the main clips, like music.

There is one overlay row. Lining up two cameras by their sound is not built yet.

=== fit-fill-blur
title: Fit, Fill or Blur
section: Picture
keywords: fit, fill, blur, fit to frame, black bars, bars, borders, crop, edges cut, background, blurred background, vertical, portrait photo
related: shape, look, crop-position

When a clip has a different shape than your video (for example an upright phone video in a wide video), you choose how it sits in the frame. Select the clip, tap **Look** and find **Fit to frame**:

- **Fit**: you see the whole picture, with bars where it does not fill the frame.
- **Fill**: it fills the whole frame, and the edges that stick out are cut off. Which part stays is set with **Position** under **Crop** (the middle unless you move it).
- **Blur**: you see the whole picture over a blurred, bigger copy of itself. No black bars.

Photos of another shape get **Blur** by themselves when you add them.

=== freeze-frame
title: Freeze frame
section: Picture
keywords: freeze, freeze frame, still, pause picture, hold, stop motion, still image
related: split, timeline

A freeze frame holds the picture still for 2 seconds.

1. Move the playhead onto the moment you want to hold, inside a video clip.
2. Select the clip. On a computer tap **Freeze**. On a phone tap **More**, then **Freeze frame**.

The clip is split there and a still picture goes in between. Undo takes it away again.

=== shape
title: Shape: upright, wide or square
section: Picture
keywords: shape, aspect, aspect ratio, ratio, orientation, rotate, portrait, landscape, vertical, horizontal, square, 9:16, 16:9, 1:1, 4:5, tiktok, reels, shorts, instagram, youtube
related: fit-fill-blur, export

**Shape** is the button next to **Export** at the top. It sets the shape of the whole video:

- **Original (as the first clip)**: the same shape as your first clip.
- **9:16 upright**: for phones, stories, reels and shorts.
- **4:5 upright**: a little less tall, good for feeds.
- **16:9 wide**: for TV, computers and YouTube.
- **1:1 square**.

Clips of another shape are fitted in. Choose how for each clip with **Fit**, **Fill** or **Blur** under **Look**, and which part stays in the frame with **Crop** (see "Crop, rotate and position the picture"). You can change the shape any time; nothing is lost.

=== volume-and-mute
title: Volume and mute
section: Sound
keywords: volume, loud, louder, quiet, quieter, soft, mute, silent, no sound, sound off, audio level, too loud
related: music, clean-up-sound, lower-music, sound-tracks

Select a video clip and tap **Sound**.

- **Volume** goes from 0% (silent) to 200% (twice as loud).
- **Mute** switches the clip's sound off completely.

**Apply to all clips** (in the same panel) gives every other video clip this clip's volume, mute and sound settings.

Music and other sounds on the sound tracks have their own **Volume**: select them on the timeline.

To mute the sound of all video clips at once, tap **Video** on the left of the timeline (on a phone, the film icon) and switch on **Mute**. Each sound track has a **Volume** and **Mute** of its own there too.

=== clean-up-sound
title: Clean up sound
section: Sound
keywords: clean up sound, cleanup, clean, noise, background noise, hiss, hum, rumble, wind, denoise, noise reduction, improve audio, better sound, voice, echo
related: voice-focus, preparing-sound, hear-a-sample, volume-and-mute

**Clean up sound** lowers steady background noise (hiss, hum, a fan, rumble) and makes voices more even in loudness.

Select a video clip, tap **Sound** and switch on **Clean up sound**.

Good to know:

- The sound is processed in the background a few seconds after you switch it on (see **Preparing sound** below). Then the preview plays it exactly as the export will.
- Until then the preview plays a light version.
- A small wand badge on the clip shows that clean-up is on.
- It does not remove music or other people talking. For speech with music behind it, try **Voice focus**.

=== voice-focus
title: Voice focus
section: Sound
keywords: voice focus, voice, speech, talking, dialogue, clearer voice, music behind voice, background music, reduce music, isolate voice, podcast, interview
related: clean-up-sound, hear-a-sample, sound-from, voice-separation

**Voice focus** makes speech clearer and tones down music, rumble and hiss behind it.

Select a video clip, tap **Sound** and move the **Voice focus** slider. Start around 50%, then listen.

- It works best when the music is spread wide in stereo and the voice is in the middle.
- On a phone recording with music in the room, it mostly cuts bass and hiss.
- It cannot remove music completely. Separating voice and music needs AI, which is coming later.
- Once the sound is processed (a few seconds), the preview plays it in full, exactly as the export will. Until then you hear part of it.

A small speaking-head badge on the clip shows that voice focus is on.

=== sound-from
title: Sound from: track or side
section: Sound
keywords: sound from, track, audio track, left, right, left only, right only, both sides, side, channel, stereo, microphone, mic, two mics, interview, lavalier
related: voice-focus, clean-up-sound, remove-silences

Some recordings keep each microphone on its own side (left or right) or on its own sound track. **Sound from** lets a clip use just one of them.

Select the video clip and tap **Sound**. **Sound from** only shows up when the file has more than one track or side.

- **Both sides**: normal.
- **Left only** or **Right only**: that side is heard on both speakers.
- **Track 1**, **Track 2**…: which sound track of the file to use.

Not sure? Leave it on **Both sides**. If one person sounds loud and the other faint, try **Left only** and **Right only** and listen.

=== hear-a-sample
title: Hear a sample and Hear original
section: Sound
keywords: hear a sample, sample, hear original, original, compare, listen, preview sound, before and after, with changes, test
related: clean-up-sound, voice-focus, preparing-sound

The preview plays the full sound changes once the sound is processed (see Preparing sound). Two buttons help you hear the difference. They show up in **Sound** once **Clean up sound**, **Voice focus** or a side is chosen.

- **Hear original** switches the preview's sound changes off while it is on. Turn it on and off while the video plays to compare.
- **Hear a sample** makes 15 seconds around the playhead exactly the way the export will sound. Switch between **With changes** and **Original** while it plays.

Nothing you do here changes your video.

=== preparing-sound
title: Preparing sound
section: Sound
keywords: preparing sound, processing sound, processing, sound ready, wait, progress, percent, stop, process sound, try again, slow, preview sound, hear changes, instant
related: clean-up-sound, voice-focus, hear-a-sample

When you switch on **Clean up sound** or move **Voice focus**, ClipForge works out the clip's new sound in the background with the same steps as the export. You don't have to wait for the export to hear your changes.

- While it works, the clip on the timeline shows a thin bar and "Sound 40%", the **Sound** panel shows "Processing sound… 40%", and the preview says "Preparing sound…". Meanwhile you hear a light version.
- When it is done the panel says "Sound ready" and the preview plays the processed sound, in step with the picture, at the clip's speed and volume.
- **Hear original** switches back to the untouched sound at once, to compare.
- **Stop** stops it; **Process sound** starts it again. If it fails, **Try again**. The export still does the full sound either way.
- It starts when you stop moving a slider, and works less while the video plays, so playback stays smooth. A long recording takes a little while (roughly one or two seconds per minute of sound on a computer, longer on a phone).
- The processed sound is kept on this device and used again by the export, which makes exporting faster. Old ones are deleted by themselves when they take up too much room.

=== remove-silences
title: Remove silences (podcasts)
section: Sound
keywords: remove silences, silence, silent, quiet parts, gaps, pauses, dead air, podcast, ums, tighten, jump cut, sensitivity, noisy, background noise, nothing found
related: split, sound-from, captions, separate-sound, voice-recorded-separately

**Remove silences** finds the quiet gaps in a clip and cuts them out. Handy for podcasts and talking videos.

1. Select the clip and tap **Sound**.
2. Tap **Remove silences**. ClipForge listens for quiet parts.
3. It tells you how many gaps it found and how much shorter the clip gets. Tap the button that says how many it removes, for example **Remove 5 gaps**.

A little silence is kept on each side so speech does not sound chopped. Undo brings everything back. The pieces keep the clip's settings.

ClipForge first measures the background noise of the recording and counts as silence only what is a few dB above it, so it also works on noisy phone recordings. If it cuts too much or too little, change **Sensitivity** in the same window: **Fewer cuts**, **Automatic** or **More cuts**. The count updates at once.

If the clip's sound was separated to a sound track, the sound is cut together with the picture.

If a voice recorded separately is lined up with the clip, ClipForge listens to that voice (to all of them when there are several speakers) instead of the muted camera, and cuts the voice together with the picture.

=== music
title: Adding music, volume and fades
section: Music
keywords: music, song, soundtrack, audio, background music, mp3, add music, fade, fade in, fade out, music volume, music too loud
related: lower-music, volume-and-mute, importing, sound-tracks, voice-recorded-separately

Tap **Music**. **My files** picks a sound file on your device; ClipForge then asks what it is: **Voice for the video** (see Voice recorded separately), **Music** or **Sound effect**. **Find free music** searches music you may use for free (see Find free music). It starts at the playhead, on the Music track (or on the track of the sound you have selected). If something already plays there, it goes on another sound track.

Select the music on the timeline to change it:

- **Volume**: how loud the music is.
- **Fade in** and **Fade out**: the music starts and ends gently.
- Drag it to move it, or drag its ends to trim it.

Music that runs past the end of your video is greyed out and fades out where the video ends.

Music can sit on any of up to three sound tracks: see Sound tracks.

To make the music end exactly with the video, tap **Fit music to video** (see Fit music to video).

=== fit-music
title: Fit music to video
section: Music
keywords: fit music, fit music to video, music too long, music too short, end with the video, loop, repeat, repeat music, loop music, stop looping, music ends early, trim music
related: music, music-credits, sound-tracks

Select the music on the timeline and tap **Fit music to video**.

- Music that is longer than the video is cut so it ends exactly where the video ends, with a 2-second fade-out.
- Music that is shorter asks whether to **Loop** it: the part you hear plays again and again until the video ends, with a short cross-fade at each repeat, and fades out at the end. On the timeline it shows a repeat sign and thin lines where it starts again.
- If another sound comes later on the same track, the music stops just before it.

It is one step: **Undo** takes it back, and the History shows "Fitted music to the video" or "Looped music to fit the video". **Stop looping** makes looped music play once again. Dragging the end of looped music makes it repeat for longer or shorter.

Cut your video first: the music fits the video as it is when you tap the button.

=== free-music
title: Find free music
section: Music
keywords: free music, find music, search music, music library, royalty free, no copyright, creative commons, cc0, cc by, openverse, jamendo, freesound, download music, songs, background music
related: music, music-credits, fit-music

Tap **Music**, then **Find free music**. Type what you want, for example "happy ukulele" or "calm piano", and search.

- Each result shows its title, artist, length and licence. Tap the play button to listen (it plays from the internet).
- Tap **Use** to download it into ClipForge and add it at the playhead. It stays with your project and works offline from then on.
- The music comes from Openverse, a free catalogue of openly licensed music (Jamendo, Freesound, Wikimedia Commons and others).
- Only music you may use in videos you share is shown: CC0 and public domain (no credit needed), CC BY and CC BY-SA (credit the artist). **Also show music with stricter licences** adds "non-commercial" (NC: not in posts that earn money) and "no changes" (ND: may not be put under a video) music; use those only if you know the licence allows what you do.
- ClipForge keeps the licence and the credit with the music: see Music credits.

Without an internet connection the search says so; your own music files still work.

Licences are given by the people who uploaded the music. ClipForge cannot check them; the page each song comes from (in its credit) has the details.

=== music-credits
title: Music credits
section: Music
keywords: credits, credit, attribution, give credit, artist, licence, license, cc by, copy credits, add credits, end credits, caption
related: free-music, music, titles

Music from **Find free music** remembers its title, artist, licence and where it came from. Select it on the timeline to see **Credits**.

- **Copy credits** copies a line for each piece of music (with links) to paste into your post's description.
- **Add credits at the end** puts a small caption with the credits over the last 4 seconds of your video. Edit it like any title.

=== lower-music
title: Lower music under speech
section: Music
keywords: lower music under speech, ducking, duck, music too loud, voice over, talking over music, quieter music, background music
related: music, voice-focus, volume-and-mute, sound-tracks

Lowering under speech makes a sound track quieter while someone talks: while the video clips have their own sound, or while a track that holds speech (the Voice track) plays. When they stop, the track comes back up.

It is set per track. Select a piece of music and switch on Lower Music under speech, or open the track's menu (tap its name on the left of the timeline) and switch on **Lower this track under speech**. With nothing selected, **Lower music under speech** switches it for every track except the Voice track.

**This track is speech** in the track menu decides which tracks count as someone talking. A Voice track does from the start.

It is applied when you export, so you will not hear it in the preview.

=== sound-tracks
title: Sound tracks
section: Music
keywords: sound track, sound tracks, track, tracks, two sound tracks, three tracks, layers, voice track, music track, effects, sound effects, multitrack, mute track, solo, track volume, rename track, delete track, add sound track
related: separate-sound, music, lower-music, volume-and-mute, voice-recorded-separately

Besides the sound of your video clips, a project can have up to three sound tracks, each a row on the timeline: for example **Voice**, **Music** and **Effects**. Sounds on different tracks play at the same time; on one track they follow each other and never overlap.

- Add a track: tap the button above the rows on the left of the timeline (**Sound track** on a computer). Adding music or separating a clip's sound makes one by itself when needed.
- Track menu: tap a track's name on the left. There you can rename it and set its **Volume**, **Mute**, **Solo** (hear only this track, also in the export), **Lower this track under speech** and **This track is speech**. **Delete track** works once the track is empty.
- Move a sound to another track: drag it up or down, or pick the track under **Track** in its settings. When the place is taken, it goes to the nearest free spot.
- Video: the first name on the left is the sound of the video clips. You can mute it or turn it down there.

=== separate-sound
title: Separate sound from video
section: Music
keywords: separate sound, unlink audio, detach audio, split audio, l-cut, j-cut, sound before picture, voice track, podcast, mute video sound, extract audio, put back
related: sound-tracks, remove-silences, clean-up-sound, lower-music, voice-recorded-separately

**Separate sound to track…** (select a video clip, tap **Sound**) moves the clip's sound to a sound track, usually the Voice track. The clip itself is muted. The sound keeps the clip's speed, volume, **Clean up sound** and **Voice focus**.

Until you move the sound yourself, it follows its clip: trimming, moving, splitting and **Remove silences** change the sound too, and deleting the clip deletes its sound.

Drag the sound a little to the left or right to let it start before or after the picture (editors call that a J-cut or an L-cut). From then on it stays where you put it.

**Put it back on the clip** (in the clip's **Sound**) or **Put back on the clip** (in the sound's settings) undoes the separation.

For a podcast: separate the sound of all clips (tick Also the other clips with sound), add music on the Music track and switch on Lower Music under speech for it.

Was the voice recorded on its own file (a microphone, Zoom)? See Voice recorded separately.

=== voice-recorded-separately
title: Voice recorded separately (microphone, Zoom)
section: Music
keywords: separate microphone, voice, microphone, mic, external audio, zoom, zoom recording, podcast, interview, sync, synchronise, line up, align, lip sync, out of sync, nudge, link, unlink, mute camera, camera sound, speaker, two files, audio file, m4a, what is this sound, sound effect
related: separate-sound, sound-tracks, remove-silences, music, clean-up-sound

Sometimes the voice is recorded on its own file while the camera films: a separate microphone, a recorder, or Zoom, which saves one video and one sound file per person. ClipForge can put such a voice under the picture and keep it there while you cut.

1. Add the video first, then the sound file: drop it on the window, or tap **Music** and **My files**.
2. ClipForge asks what the sound is. Choose **Voice for the video** (it is chosen for you when the file name or its length says so), type who speaks, and tap **Add and line up**.
3. ClipForge compares the voice with the sound the camera recorded and lines the two up. The voice gets its own track, named after the speaker, and the camera's own sound is muted (**Mute the camera's own sound**).

Several people? Add one file per person; each gets its own track. A project has three sound tracks, so with a Music track two voices fit.

From then on the voice follows its clip: trimming, splitting, speed, **Remove silences** and deleting change the voice too, in the same Undo step. **Remove silences** listens to the voice, not to the muted camera. **Clean up sound** and **Voice focus** work on the voice as on any clip.

## When it could not be lined up

If the camera has no sound, or the two sounds are too different, ClipForge says so, puts the voice at the start of the clip and leaves the camera's sound on. Select the voice and use the buttons under **Nudge the voice** (1 frame, 10 ms, 100 ms, earlier or later) until lips and voice match, then switch on **Mute the camera's own sound**. **Line up with the video again** tries the comparison once more.

## Good to know

- Voice starts later than the camera, or ends earlier: there is simply silence where the voice file has nothing.
- If two recorders run at slightly different speeds, the voice slowly drifts. ClipForge tells you and picks the best average; it does not stretch sound.
- **Unlink** leaves the voice where it is, but it no longer follows the picture.
- A sound that is already in the project as music: select it and choose **This is a voice: line up with the video**. On a computer: Edit, Sound, **Add voice recording…** and **Line up with the video**.

=== keyboard-shortcuts
title: Keyboard shortcuts
section: Computer
keywords: keyboard, shortcuts, keys, hotkeys, space, play, pause, ctrl z, cmd z, undo, redo, delete key, arrow keys, frame by frame, home, end, j k l, jkl, shuttle, in point, out point, duplicate, ctrl d, copy settings, paste settings, zoom, f1, quit, export
related: menus, undo-and-saving, split, timeline, snapping

On a Mac use **Cmd** where this says Ctrl. Every shortcut is also in the menu bar at the top, next to its menu item (see **Menus**).

## Playing
- **Space**: play or pause.
- **J**: play backwards, **K**: stop, **L**: play forwards. Playing backwards jumps back in small steps, because video players cannot play smoothly in reverse.
- **Left** and **Right**: one frame back or forward (a frame of the clip under the playhead, so 25 or 60 a second if that is how it was filmed). With **Shift**: one second.
- **Home** and **End**: go to the start or the end.
- **+** and **-**: zoom the timeline in and out.

## Editing
- **S**: split at the playhead.
- **I** and **O**: the selected clip starts or ends at the playhead (the part before or after it is trimmed away).
- **Delete** or **Backspace**: delete what is selected.
- **Ctrl+D**: duplicate the selected clip or title.
- **Ctrl+Shift+C** and **Ctrl+Shift+V**: copy and paste the settings of a clip.
- **Ctrl+Z**: undo. **Ctrl+Shift+Z** or **Ctrl+Y**: redo.
- **Ctrl+Shift+H**: show or hide the History.
- **Ctrl+E**: export.
- Hold **Alt** (Option on a Mac) while dragging on the timeline: no snapping for that drag.

## Everywhere
- **F1** (Windows and Linux) or **Cmd+?** (Mac): this help.
- **Ctrl+N** and **Ctrl+O** on the project list: new project, open a project file.
- Quit: **Alt+F4** on Windows, **Ctrl+Q** on Linux, **Cmd+Q** on a Mac.
- **Esc**: closes the tour.

Shortcuts are off while you type in a text box.

=== claude-control

On Windows and Mac you can ask Claude, the AI assistant, to edit your ClipForge projects in plain words. For example: "Cut the silences out of my Podcast project and export it upright."

- Give it full file locations, like C:\Users\me\Videos\clip.mp4.
- Setting it up takes a few steps (installing FFmpeg and connecting Claude Desktop). They are in the README file that comes with ClipForge.
